Emergency: No Heat Or A Boiler Problem Right Now

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches. For any other boiler failure, such as no heat, water on the floor or a boiler that is shutting off and on, call now. Cold houses lose heat fast, and pipes can freeze if temperatures drop.

While you wait, check that the thermostat is set to heat, the boiler switch is on and the breaker has not tripped. If your boiler runs on oil, check the tank. If it is gas, make sure the gas valve is open.

Safety first

Never ignore a gas smell, a yellow flame, soot around the boiler or a carbon monoxide alarm. Leave the house and call emergency services. Use working carbon monoxide detectors on every level.

Common Boiler Problems

No heat or no hot water

Failed ignition, a bad circulator pump, a tripped limit switch or an empty oil tank are common causes.

Low or high pressure

Low pressure means a leak or air in the system. High pressure points to a failed expansion tank or relief valve.

Banging and gurgling

Air in the loop or buildup in the boiler makes noise. Bleeding and flushing often help.

Leaks

Water around the boiler may come from a relief valve, a fitting or the boiler itself. A leaking boiler body is usually replaced.

Short cycling

A boiler that turns on and off rapidly is wasting fuel and wearing out. Oversizing and failed controls are common causes.

What to do before the plumber arrives

A few steps keep you safe and make the visit faster. None of them involve opening the boiler.

  • Turn the thermostat down so the boiler stops calling for heat.
  • If you smell gas or oil fumes, leave the house and call 911 and your fuel supplier or gas utility.
  • Check that carbon monoxide alarms are working and do not silence them.
  • Do not relight a pilot or reset a lockout repeatedly.
  • Keep the boiler area clear and note any error code or noise.

Heat Diagnosis: What We Check

We start with the simplest checks and work toward the complex. The thermostat, power, fuel, ignition, pressure, circulators and zone valves are tested in order. Most no heat calls are traced to one failed part rather than the whole boiler.

Repair Or Replace A Boiler

Boilers are long lived, but not forever. A failed part on a sound boiler is a reasonable repair. A cracked heat exchanger, a leaking body or repeated expensive repairs point toward replacement. We explain the trade off clearly and give you the price before any work begins.

If your boiler also makes domestic hot water, a failure can leave you without both heat and showers. We can discuss water heater replacement if the two systems are linked and one is failing.

Repair makes sense when

  • One part has failed
  • The boiler is otherwise sound
  • Repairs have been infrequent
  • The system heats evenly

Replacement makes sense when

  • The heat exchanger or body is leaking
  • Repairs are frequent and costly
  • Efficiency has dropped badly
  • Parts are no longer available

Heating A Sandwich Home Through A Cape Cod Winter

Sandwich winters bring nor'easters and cold snaps, and the Town of Sandwich Department of Public Works publishes winter preparation information on storms, plowing and hydrants. Its page does not cover frozen pipes, so the basics fall to the homeowner. Keep a trickle of water in exposed lines during a hard freeze and open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls.

Natural gas customers in Sandwich are served by National Grid, and not every home is on the gas network. If you are unsure of your fuel or your meter, we can sort it out on the visit. For a gas smell, leave and call from outside.

Sources: Town of Sandwich Department of Public Works, Winter Preparation, and the Cape Light Compact.

Preventing The Next No Heat Call

A boiler checked before winter fails less often. Annual service covers the burner, controls, pressure and the expansion tank. Bleed radiators if they are cold at the top. Keep the area around the boiler clear, and keep carbon monoxide detectors working. If your system is old, ask us what we see and what to expect.
