Emergency First Steps For A Failing Disposal

If your disposal is humming, smoking or leaking, switch off the wall switch and unplug it or turn off its breaker before anything else. Never put your hand into a disposal, even if it looks off. Sinks that fill with water and will not drain should be left alone until the cause is known.

If water is dripping from the unit into the cabinet, place a bowl under it and call. A leaking disposal can ruin a cabinet floor in a short time.

Safety comes first

Use tongs or pliers to remove an object you can see. Never reach in by hand. A hex key turned from the bottom of many units can free a jam, but only with the power off.

Why Disposals Fail

Jams

Bones, fruit pits, utensils and fibrous scraps lock the flywheel. The unit hums but does not spin.

Overload trips

A tripped reset button on the bottom of the unit means the motor overheated. It may be a one time jam or a worn motor.

Leaks

Leaks come from the sink flange, the drain pipe connection or a cracked housing. A cracked housing means replacement.

Clogged drain line

A sink that fills with water may have a clog past the disposal, not a failed disposal.

Dead motor

No hum, no reset response and power at the outlet usually mean the motor has failed.

Repair Versus Replacement

A jam or a loose connection is a quick fix. A leaking housing, a seized motor or a disposal that has failed more than once is better replaced. Today's disposals vary in motor strength, noise level and features, and we help you choose one that matches how you cook.

We handle both the repair and the install, including the electrical connection to the unit, the dishwasher drain knockout and a new sink flange where needed.

Disposals, Septic Systems And Sandwich Properties

Whether to use a disposal at all depends partly on how your home handles wastewater. Sandwich Health Department oversees water and wastewater matters, and some Sandwich homes are on Title 5 septic systems. Food scraps add solids to a tank and can shorten the time between pumpings.

If your home is on septic, ask the Health Department or your septic professional whether a disposal suits your system. We will install one if you decide to, and tell you honestly if we think you should reconsider.

Source: Town of Sandwich Health Department, Water and Wastewater. Title 5 is 310 CMR 15.000.

What Not To Put Down A Disposal

Keep out of the disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts
  • Bones, corn husks and celery strings
  • Eggshell membranes in bulk
  • Pasta and rice that swell

Fine in small amounts

  • Soft food scraps
  • Small bits of cooked vegetables
  • Citrus rind in small pieces
  • Ice cubes occasionally
  • Small plate scrapings with running cold water

Keeping Your Disposal Running

Run cold water before, during and for a few seconds after grinding. Feed scraps in gradually. Do not overload the unit. A cleaning cycle with ice and a citrus peel freshens it, though it does not replace proper use. If the unit smells, the problem is usually food film in the chamber and the drain line.

If the sink is slow and you do not have a disposal problem, the line may need clearing. See drain cleaning for kitchen lines.
