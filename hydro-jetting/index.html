When Hydro Jetting Is The Right Emergency Call

If wastewater is rising in a tub, floor drain or toilet and a drain snake only bought you a few weeks, call now. Repeat blockages usually mean the pipe wall is coated, not that a single wad of paper is stuck. A cable punches a hole through the middle of the buildup and leaves the rest clinging to the pipe.

Hydro jetting uses a hose fitted with a nozzle that fires water backward and sideways at high pressure. The jets cut grease, soap scum and fine roots off the pipe wall, then flush the debris down and out. The goal is a pipe that is actually clean, not one with a pinhole through the sludge.

Stop using water first

Running a washer or taking a shower while a main line is backing up pushes more water into a pipe that has nowhere to send it. Hold off on laundry and dishes until the line is cleared. If sewage is reaching a basement floor, keep people and pets away from it and call right away.

How Jetting Differs From Snaking

Both methods have a place, and a good plumber chooses based on what the line is telling them. A cable machine is fast and effective for a fresh stoppage near a fixture. Jetting is the better tool when the blockage keeps returning or the line has years of buildup in it.

Our Jetting Process From Call To Clean Line

We do not run a jetter into a pipe blind. A pipe that has collapsed or separated can be damaged by high pressure, so the line is checked first and the method is chosen from what the camera shows.

1

Locate the cleanout

We find the access point, usually a cleanout near the foundation or in the basement, so the hose enters the line cleanly.

2

Camera the line

A small camera shows the buildup, root intrusion and the pipe material before any pressure is applied.

3

Jet the length

The nozzle works from the cleanout toward the street or tank, then is pulled back to rinse the wall.

4

Verify on video

We run the camera again so you can see the pipe wall afterward, and we note any damage the cleaning revealed.

Signs Your Line Needs Jetting Instead Of Another Snake

Most homeowners do not call about the buildup. They call about what it does. These are the patterns that point toward jetting.

  • The same drain slows down again within weeks or months of being cleared.
  • A kitchen line gurgles after the dishwasher runs, a sign of grease narrowing the pipe.
  • More than one fixture drains slowly at the same time.
  • Toilets bubble when the washing machine empties.
  • A previous camera inspection showed roots or scale along the wall.

What Jetting Will Not Fix

Jetting cleans a pipe. It does not repair one. If the camera shows a cracked section, a belly that holds water or a joint that has pulled apart, cleaning only buys time. In that case we show you the footage and talk through the options, which may include sewer line repair on the damaged section.

It also is not a septic tank service. Jetting addresses the house sewer line between your fixtures and the street or the tank. Pumping and inspecting a septic tank is a separate job.

Sandwich Notes: Sewer Or Septic On Your Street?

Before booking any line work in Sandwich, it helps to know whether your home connects to a municipal sewer or a Title 5 septic system. The Town of Sandwich Health Department keeps a Water and Wastewater section, and the Health Department is the place to ask whether your street has sewer or a septic system.

That answer changes what we clear. On a septic property the line runs to a tank and field, and we are careful about where the flushed debris ends up. Tell the dispatcher what you know and we will plan around it.

Source: Town of Sandwich Health Department, Water and Wastewater section. Title 5 is 310 CMR 15.000.

Keeping The Line Clean After We Leave

A freshly jetted line stays clear longer when the habits feeding it change. Cooking grease belongs in a can in the trash, not down the sink. Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like paper. Hair catchers in showers cost very little and catch what jetting later has to remove.

What feeds buildup

  • Pouring warm grease down the kitchen sink
  • Flushing wipes and paper towels
  • Coffee grounds in the disposal
  • Skipping the shower hair catcher

What keeps a line clear

  • Wiping pans before washing
  • Flushing only waste and toilet paper
  • Compost or trash for grounds
  • A drain screen in every tub and shower
