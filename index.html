Local plumbing

Plumbing on the Upper Cape has its own quirks

Sandwich sits on the Cape Cod aquifer, and most homes here get water from the Sandwich Water District, which treats it with sodium hydroxide for corrosion control. That is a normal and regulated step, and according to its published water quality report, the district reports no detectable PFAS6 in 2025. If you see blue-green stains or have pinhole leaks in copper, ask us to look at the piping rather than guessing.

Many houses here are second homes or seasonal rentals. A home that sits closed through a cold snap is exactly where frozen and split pipes show up, and a leak can run for days before anyone notices. If you manage a vacation property, a leak detection visit is cheaper than a ruined floor.

Older neighborhoods near Route 6A and Shawme Pond have homes with decades of added-on plumbing, so a simple fixture swap sometimes turns up old galvanized or mixed metals. We tell you what we find and what it means before you decide anything.
