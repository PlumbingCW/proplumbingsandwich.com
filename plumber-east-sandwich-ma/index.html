Plumbing Help In East Sandwich

East Sandwich is a village in the northeastern part of Sandwich. Cape Cod Bay lies to the north, Sandwich Village to the west, Route 6 to the south and Barnstable to the east. Route 6A is the main road through it.

Two things stand out in the public record. A good number of homes are on private wells instead of town water, and the census counts a large share of units as vacant. This page covers both.

Private Wells And Town Water

The 2018 town plan says about 2,500 households rely on private wells, primarily in East Sandwich, and that about a quarter of Sandwich homes are not served by public water lines, mostly in the eastern part of town. Those numbers are several years old, so check your own address with the Sandwich Water District or the Health Department.

On a well, the plumber's side is the house: pipes, fixtures, the water heater and the plumbing around a pressure tank. The well pump and the well itself are usually a well contractor's work. Ask when you call who covers what.

If you want to treat well water, ask the Sandwich Health Department what testing it recommends first, then choose equipment based on the results. Filter and softener hookups are covered under plumbing installation.

Low or uneven pressure

On a well system the cause can be the pump, the pressure tank or the house piping. Working out which one first can save money.

Stains and scale

Rust marks or white buildup can have several causes. A water test tells you more than guessing does.

Pressure tank plumbing

Valves, gauges and connections around the tank wear out like any other part of the system.

Treatment hookups

Whole-house filters and softeners need the right shutoffs and bypass valves so the system can be serviced.

Source: Open Space and Recreation Plan (2018).

Homes That Sit Empty

Freezing weather is hardest on a house with nobody in it. A pipe can split and run for days before anyone sees it. If you own a home you only use part of the year, a few habits help. See burst pipe repair for what to do if a pipe fails and leak detection for finding slow leaks.

  • Shut off the water at the main when you leave for a long stretch, and ask a plumber how to drain the lines.
  • Keep the heat on at a safe minimum if you stay connected to heating, and ask someone to look in regularly.
  • Check the water heater and the area around it before you leave and when you return.
  • When you reopen the house in spring, turn the water on slowly and look for drips at fixtures and valves.

Coming back to a wet house

If you open a closed-up home and find water on the floor, shut off the main first, then call. Photograph the damage before cleanup. Keep the main off until a plumber has looked at the source.

Many East Sandwich homes are on private wells, but not all. The Sandwich Water District or your water bill can tell you which supply serves your address.

Winterizing means shutting off and draining the water system so pipes cannot freeze. Ask about it when you call, and ask for a leak check when you reopen the house in spring.

The 2009 town plan says the Old Kings Highway Regional Historic District reviews new development and expansions north of Route 6. Whether a plumbing project touches that review depends on whether it changes the outside of the house, so ask the Building Department before work begins.

The state limits plumbing permits to licensed plumbers, and the local plumbing inspector issues them. For East Sandwich addresses that is the Sandwich Building Department, which can tell you whether a planned job needs a permit.
