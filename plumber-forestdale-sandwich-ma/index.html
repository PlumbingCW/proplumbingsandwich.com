Plumbing Help For Forestdale Homes

Forestdale is a village in the southern part of Sandwich, with Mashpee to its south and Joint Base Cape Cod to its west. Route 130, also called Forestdale Road, is the main road through it. Most of what is here is housing, so this page is written for homeowners dealing with everyday plumbing: drains, toilets, water heaters, faucets and leaks.

Call the number on this page and describe the problem. A plumber can tell you whether it sounds like something that can wait for a scheduled visit or something that needs attention today.

Everyday Plumbing In Family Homes

A family home asks a lot of its plumbing at once. These are the areas homeowners usually want help with.

Water heaters

Showers, laundry and dishes can all run hot water at the same time. If a tank leaks, rusts at the base or cannot keep up, see water heater repair and water heater replacement.

Drains and toilets

Slow tubs, gurgling toilets and kitchen sinks that back up happen in any home. See drain cleaning and toilet repair.

Faucets and fixtures

A dripping faucet or a running toilet wastes water quietly. A small repair now can avoid a bigger bill later. See faucet repair.

Hidden leaks

A water bill that climbs with no change in habits can point to a leak you cannot see. Leak detection looks for it without opening walls blindly.

Septic, Water And Who Decides What

Sandwich's own planning documents describe a town that relies on private septic systems and has no public sewer. Those documents are older, and the town has kept studying wastewater options, so do not assume anything about your street. Find out from the Sandwich Health Department if your street is sewered or on a Title 5 septic system.

This matters in practical ways. A septic system does not like grease, wipes or harsh drain chemicals, and a new bathroom or kitchen adds load. A plumber can clear a clogged line, but the tank and leach field are a separate job for a septic professional.

On water, the 2018 town plan says the Sandwich Water District serves about three-quarters of the town's land area. If your home is on district water, the district's 2025 report says it adds sodium hydroxide to control corrosion of household plumbing. Its FAQ suggests running a tap for 30 seconds to 2 minutes after water has sat for a long time. If you are not sure which supply you have, ask the district.

Sources: Town of Sandwich Local Comprehensive Plan (2009), Open Space and Recreation Plan (2018) and the Sandwich Water District 2025 water quality report.

What To Do Before The Plumber Arrives

These steps are general advice for any home and they help in an emergency.

1

Find the main shutoff

Know where the main shutoff valve is and make sure it turns. Everyone in the house should know where it is.

2

Stop the water

For a fixture leak, close the small valve under the sink or behind the toilet first. If water keeps running, close the main.

3

Protect and photograph

Move rugs and valuables out of the wet area. Take photos before cleanup in case you file an insurance claim.

4

Call and describe

Give the street, what you see and when it started. That helps the plumber decide how to help you.

If you smell gas, do not call a plumber first

Leave the home, then call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches or use a phone inside. Plumbing work can wait until the gas company says the home is safe.
