Plumbing In The Historic Village

Sandwich Village is what the town's plans call the historic village center. The 2009 Local Comprehensive Plan describes it as about 100 acres and 151 parcels, centered on Jarves, Pleasant and Main Streets and Route 6A, and calls it the heart and soul of the community.

Its ZIP code is 02563. This page covers plumbing in the village's houses, converted buildings and small commercial spaces.

Older Houses, Honest Answers

A house built in the 1800s may have been repiped, added to and repaired many times. The only way to know what is behind a wall is to look. A plumber can open an access panel or run a camera, then tell you what is there before you decide on repairs.

We do not assume an older house has any particular pipe or problem. These are the jobs that usually come up when someone owns or renovates an older home.

Renovation plumbing

Moving a sink, adding a bathroom or updating a kitchen starts with what is already in place. See plumbing installation.

Repiping

When pipes have reached the end of their life, planning access in an older house takes care. See repiping.

Camera inspection

A camera shows the inside of a drain or sewer line so repairs start from facts. See sewer inspection.

Drains

Slow drains in older buildings can come from the line, the fixture or buildup inside the pipe. See drain cleaning.

Homes That Became Something Else

The 2009 town plan says several village homes are being converted into offices, lodging and apartments. A building used that way has more fixtures, more hot water demand and drains that must work all season. Shops and rentals are covered on our commercial plumbing page.

The same plan says the lack of public wastewater treatment limits renovation, expansion and infill in the village. If you plan to add fixtures or change how a building is used, ask the Sandwich Health Department and Building Department early.

Source: Town of Sandwich Local Comprehensive Plan (2009).

Historic District Questions

The 2009 plan says the National Historic District includes everything north of Route 6, and that the Old Kings Highway Regional Historic District reviews new development and expansions there. Interior plumbing is out of sight, but a new exterior vent, a relocated meter or an addition can be visible from the street.

If a plumbing project changes the outside of the building, ask the Sandwich Building Department whether it needs review before work begins.

Before you open a wall

Cutting into an old wall can reveal pipes, vents and wiring nobody has seen in decades. Stop and call before going further, and ask the Building Department whether the job needs a permit.
