Signs Your Pipes Need Replacing

One leak is a repair. A pattern of leaks is a warning. These signs usually mean the whole system is aging out.

  • Rust-colored or brown water at the tap, especially after the water sits.
  • Weak pressure at several fixtures even though the supply is good.
  • Pinhole leaks that keep appearing in different places.
  • Green or white crust on copper joints and fittings.
  • Visible corrosion on galvanized steel pipe.
  • Wall or ceiling stains that return after a repair.

PEX Or Copper

PEX is flexible plastic tubing that threads through walls with fewer cuts. That usually means less drywall damage and a faster job. It handles freeze expansion better than rigid pipe, which matters in cold New England winters.

Copper is durable and familiar and some owners prefer it for exposed runs. It costs more in materials and labor. We explain both and recommend the better match for your layout and budget instead of pushing one type for every house.

How A Repipe Works

A good repipe is planned so you are not without water longer than necessary.

1

Survey

We trace the existing lines, locate the main shutoff and decide the best route for new pipe.

2

Access

We open small access points in walls, floors or ceilings and protect the rooms we work in.

3

Replace

New lines go in, fixtures are reconnected and the old pipe is abandoned or removed.

4

Test and patch

We pressure test every joint before closing anything up, then restore access points.

Permits And Inspections

A repipe is major plumbing work. In Massachusetts it needs a permit pulled by a licensed plumber and an inspection when the work is done. Sandwich's Building Department handles plumbing inspections, so ask them for the current steps for your address.

We follow the state plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00, and we handle the permit and the inspection scheduling for our work. You can verify any plumber's license through the state's eLIPSE portal.

Repair Or Replace

Not every leaking pipe means a whole-house repipe. If the system is sound and only one section failed, we repair it. When leaks are frequent, the pipe is brittle or the water is discolored throughout the house, replacement saves repeated repair costs and water damage.

If you are not sure, start with leak detection so we know exactly where and why the water is escaping. For homes that mostly need new supply from the street, see water service line replacement.
