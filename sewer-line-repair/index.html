Sewage In The House Is An Emergency

If sewage is coming up through a floor drain, a tub or a toilet, call now. Raw sewage in a living space is a health hazard, and a damaged line will keep backing up until it is fixed. Stay out of the affected area and keep children and pets away.

A sewer line problem is different from a drain clog. A clog can be cleared. A crack, a collapsed section or a root-filled joint keeps coming back unless the pipe itself is repaired. Our job is to work out which one you have, and the camera is how we do it.

Emergency Sewer Backup: Steps To Take Right Now

Stop using water

Do not flush, run the washer or use sinks. Every drop goes into a line that cannot carry it.

Keep clear of the area

Avoid contact with standing sewage. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, and wash well afterward.

Protect electrics

If water is near outlets or appliances, shut off power only if you can reach the panel from a dry spot.

Photograph the damage

Take photos before cleanup. They help with an insurance claim.

Finding The Problem With A Camera

We do not guess where a sewer line has failed. A waterproof camera on a flexible cable runs through the line and records what it sees. The video shows cracks, offsets, roots, corrosion and bellies, and a locator tells us how deep the pipe is and where it sits in the yard.

That information decides everything. It tells us whether a small section needs fixing or the whole run, how deep and how long the excavation would be and whether a trenchless method will work. For a camera inspection by itself, see sewer inspection.

Repair Options: From Spot Fix To Full Replacement

Repair a section

  • Best when damage is limited to one joint or a short stretch
  • Smaller excavation, quicker work
  • Rest of the line must be in sound condition
  • Lower cost when it fits

Replace the line

  • Best when the pipe is failing in several places
  • Handles collapsed, crushed or heavily rooted pipe
  • Gives a continuous new pipe with proper slope
  • Higher cost but a longer-lasting result

Trenchless methods

In some cases we can fix a line without digging a long trench. Pipe lining inserts a resin-coated sleeve that hardens inside the old pipe. Pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the old one while breaking it apart. These methods work when the existing pipe is not too badly collapsed, and the camera tells us whether they will work for you.

Where the pipe has collapsed or has a serious belly, traditional excavation is usually the better answer, because the new pipe has to be set at the right slope on firm bedding.

Roots, Bellies And Old Pipe Materials

Tree roots are drawn to the moisture and nutrients in a sewer line. They enter through tiny cracks or loose joints and grow into a mass that catches paper and waste. Cutting the roots clears the line for a while, but unless the joint is repaired the roots return.

A belly is a low spot where the pipe has settled. Water and solids collect there, and the line clogs repeatedly. Cast iron lines corrode and flake inside. Clay pipe cracks at joints. Fiber conduit, sometimes called Orangeburg, softens and deforms. If your house is older, the camera will tell us which material you have and how much life it has left.

Sewer Lines In Sandwich: Public Sewer Or Septic

Before any repair, it matters whether your home is on a public sewer or a private septic system. The Town of Sandwich website does not state sewer coverage on its Water and Wastewater page, so ask the Sandwich Health Department whether your street has sewer or a Title 5 septic system. The answer changes what a failing line means and who has to approve the work.

Septic systems are governed by Title 5 of the State Environmental Code, 310 CMR 15.000, and Title 5 requires inspection under certain circumstances. Plumbing work on the house side is governed by the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and permits are issued only to licensed plumbers. Ask the Sandwich Building Department about your repair.

Cape Cod sits on a single aquifer that is the source of all its drinking water, designated by the EPA as a sole source aquifer. That is one more reason a leaking sewer or septic line is worth fixing promptly.

Sources: Town of Sandwich Health and Building Departments, Mass.gov Title 5 information, Cape Cod Commission.

What Happens On The Day

We arrive, listen to what you have seen and get the camera in. If the line is blocked, we clear it enough to run the camera, then record the video and show you what we found.

If the repair is a spot fix, we mark the location, dig and replace the section. If it needs replacement, we lay out the route, explain restoration and schedule the work. Utility locating comes first, because digging without it is neither safe nor legal. We leave the site safe and tidy.

Before we close out, we run water through the new section and confirm it drains freely, then give you the camera footage and a plain summary of what was done. If an inspection is required, we coordinate it so the trench is not backfilled before it is signed off.
