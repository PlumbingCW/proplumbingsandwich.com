Sump Pump Emergency: What To Do While Water Rises

If water is rising in the pit and the pump is silent, check that it is plugged in and that the breaker has not tripped. Lift the float by hand. If the pump starts, the float is stuck or tangled. If it does not, call us right away.

Do not wade into water if outlets or appliances are submerged. Turn off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry. Move belongings off the floor and, if water is climbing, use buckets or a wet vac to buy time.

Power outage during a storm

A standard pump stops when the power goes out, which is exactly when groundwater and rain are highest. A battery backup keeps the pit from filling when the grid fails. We can add one during a replacement.

Why Sump Pumps Fail

Stuck float switch

Debris or the pit wall can hold the float down, so the pump never starts.

Clogged intake

Silt, gravel and small stones clog the screen and strain the motor.

Worn motor

Pumps wear out after years of cycling. A pump that hums but does not move water is likely done.

Failed check valve

Water flows back into the pit after each cycle, so the pump runs constantly.

Frozen or blocked discharge

Ice or a crushed outdoor line keeps water from leaving the house.

Repair Or Replace

A stuck float or a clogged intake is a quick fix. If the pump is old, rusty or has cycled itself hot, replacement is the safer choice. We size the new pump to the pit and the inflow, set it on a stable base and fit a new check valve and discharge line. If you want redundancy, a battery backup or a second pump adds protection.

Sandwich Groundwater And Your Basement

Cape Cod sits on a sole source aquifer. The Cape Cod Commission notes that nearly all recharge comes from rain and snowmelt, and the aquifer supplies all of the Cape's drinking water. When wet seasons raise the water table, basements and crawl spaces feel it.

In spring 2018, Commission observation wells recorded record March levels, with Sandwich readings near the 80th percentile and the water table two to three feet higher than January. That is one season, not a forecast, but it shows why a working pump matters in a place where groundwater rises and falls.

Source: Cape Cod Commission, observation well news of April 2018 and its aquifer overview.

Keeping Your Pump Ready Before The Next Storm

Test the pump twice a year, and before heavy rain, by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. It should start, empty the pit and shut off. Clear debris from the pit. Check the discharge line outside and make sure it points away from the house. If your basement still floods with a working pump, you may need a different drainage solution.
