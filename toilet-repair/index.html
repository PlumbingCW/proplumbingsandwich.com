Toilet Overflowing? Do This In The Next Minute

If water is rising in the bowl, do not flush again. Lift the tank lid, push the flapper down to stop the refill, then turn the shutoff valve behind or beside the toilet clockwise until it stops. That alone usually halts an overflow in seconds.

If the water keeps coming or the valve will not turn, shut off the main water supply to the house and call right away. We answer around the clock.

Problems We Repair Most Often

Clogs and slow flushes

A toilet auger clears most stoppages. If several fixtures are slow, the problem is deeper in the line.

Leak at the base

Water around the floor often means a failed wax ring or loose closet bolts. Left alone, it can rot the subfloor.

Constant running

A worn flapper, fill valve or float wastes water and raises the bill.

Cracked bowl or tank

Hairline cracks seep. Once a tank cracks, replacement is the safe fix.

Wobbly seat on the floor

A rocking toilet breaks its seal. Resetting it with a new ring and shims restores it.

Repair Or Replace? How We Decide

Most toilet problems are inexpensive to fix. A fill valve, flapper or wax ring takes a short visit. Replacement makes sense when the porcelain is cracked, when parts for an old model are hard to find or when the toilet clogs repeatedly because of its design.

Setting A New Toilet Correctly

A new toilet is only as good as the flange it sits on. We check that the flange is solid, level with the finished floor and free of rot. A cracked flange is repaired before the toilet goes back on. The wax ring seals the joint, closet bolts are snugged evenly without cracking the porcelain, and the supply line gets a fresh connection.

We test several flushes and check the base for leaks before we leave. If you are choosing a model, ask about rough in size, since a mismatch is a common reason a new toilet does not fit.

Sandwich Water And Your Toilet

The Sandwich Water District reports adding sodium hydroxide at its wells and booster station to control corrosion of household plumbing. That is good news for pipes and fittings. It does not stop flappers and fill valves from wearing out, so a toilet that runs or hisses is still a parts issue.

A little over four in five Sandwich homes are year round residences, according to a town figure, which means a meaningful share are seasonal. A toilet that sat unused for months can have a dried wax ring or a stiff flapper, so a quick check when you reopen a seasonal home is worthwhile.

Source: Sandwich Water District 2025 Water Quality Report, and a town finance director figure reported by Cape Cod News.

Save Water With A Quick Leak Test

A silent leak from tank to bowl wastes water without a sound. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking. It is a small fix, and finding it early keeps the water bill honest.

Do it yourself

  • Swap a flapper or fill valve on a standard toilet
  • Use an auger on a simple clog
  • Tighten a loose tank bolt gently

Call a plumber

  • Water around the base of the toilet
  • Repeated clogs or gurgling in nearby drains
  • A cracked bowl or tank
  • Any overflow you cannot stop
