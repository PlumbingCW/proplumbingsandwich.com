If The Tank Is Leaking, Act Before You Diagnose

If water is pooling around the base of your water heater, call now. A leak from the tank body is not a repair job, but a leak from a fitting, valve or drain can be fixed in a single visit, and the first thing that matters is limiting the damage.

No hot water at all is urgent in a different way. Sandwich winters are cold, and a house with no hot water and no heat from a related boiler problem needs quick attention. We start by working out whether this is a simple part failure or a sign the whole unit is finished.

Emergency Steps Before The Plumber Arrives

Shut the water supply

Close the cold water valve on the pipe above the heater. Turn the handle until it stops.

Cut the fuel or power

For electric units, switch off the dedicated breaker. For gas units, turn the gas control to pilot or off.

Do not touch wet wiring

If the floor around an electric heater is wet, stay out and shut off power at the panel from a dry spot.

Protect belongings

Lift boxes and stored items off the floor. A hose from the drain valve to a floor drain can reduce pooling.

Electric Units

Electric heaters have two heating elements and two thermostats. If you get lukewarm water, one element has usually failed. If you get none, the upper element, the upper thermostat or the high limit reset has tripped or died.

Elements and thermostats are inexpensive parts, and swapping them is often a fast repair. Before we replace anything we test with a meter, because a tripped breaker or a loose connection can mimic a dead element.

Gas Units

Gas water heaters add venting and combustion to the list of things that can fail. A pilot that will not stay lit often points at a worn thermocouple. A burner that lights then shuts off can mean a dirty burner, a weak draft or a failing gas control.

If you ever smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not turn on lights or try to relight the unit. For routine failures, we check venting, combustion air and the gas connection as part of any repair.

Tankless Units

Tankless heaters bring their own failures. Error codes, ignition faults, scale on the heat exchanger and clogged inlet screens are the usual causes. Many problems clear with a descaling flush, which is why routine service matters on these units.

If you own a tankless heater and the flow is weak or the water turns cold midway through a shower, call and describe what the display says. See our tankless water heater service page for maintenance details.

Repair Or Replace: How We Decide

We give you both numbers when it is a close call and let you decide. If replacement is the answer, see water heater replacement for what that involves.

Repair makes sense when

  • The leak is at a fitting, valve or drain and not the tank body
  • A single part such as an element, thermostat or thermocouple has failed
  • The tank is otherwise sound and the unit is not near the end of its life
  • The repair costs a small fraction of a new unit

Replace makes sense when

  • The tank body is leaking or visibly corroded
  • Repairs keep stacking up on an older unit
  • Rusty water points to a tank that is failing inside
  • A new unit would cut your recurring repair calls

Water Heaters In Sandwich Homes

Sandwich draws its drinking water from groundwater wells run by the Sandwich Water District. The district reports that it adds sodium hydroxide at its wells and booster station to control corrosion of household plumbing. That is a treatment choice aimed at your pipes and fittings, and it is worth knowing when you ask how long a tank might last.

Water chemistry is only one factor in how long a heater lasts, and the district's 2025 report does not give a hardness figure. If you want to know more about the water at your tap, ask the Sandwich Water District, or test it yourself. Tell us what you find and we will factor it into whether to repair or replace.

What Happens On The Visit

We arrive, confirm the fuel type and look over the unit before touching anything. Then we test, because a guess costs you money. On electric units that means checking power at the panel and testing elements and thermostats with a meter. On gas units it means checking the pilot, the gas supply, the venting and the burner.

Once we know what failed, we explain it in plain words and give the price for the repair. If the unit is past saving, we say so and walk through replacement options. Nothing starts until you agree, and the area is cleaned up when we finish.

If a leak has already done damage, we shut the unit down safely and show you where the water went so you can document it for your insurer.

Routine Care That Extends A Heater's Life

Test the temperature and pressure relief valve once a year by lifting the lever briefly. Water should flow and stop cleanly. If it drips afterward or does not flow, the valve needs replacing.

Flushing the tank clears sediment that hardens into a crust and makes the burner or elements work harder. On tankless units a descaling flush does the same job. Set the thermostat no higher than needed, because hotter water wears the tank and the fittings faster.
