When A Failed Heater Cannot Wait

If your water heater is leaking from the tank, has failed completely or is dumping water from the relief valve, call now. A heater that has lost its tank integrity will not recover, and every hour of delay risks more water on the floor and in the walls.

Replacement is a bigger decision than repair, so we keep it simple. We confirm the problem, measure the space, price the options and install the unit that fits. You do not have to decide everything in a panic. We tell you what has to happen today and what can wait.

Emergency Replacement: What To Do Before We Arrive

1

Shut off the water

Close the cold supply valve above the heater.

2

Kill power or gas

Switch off the breaker for an electric unit, or set the gas control to off for a gas unit.

3

Drain if needed

Attach a hose to the drain valve and run it to a floor drain or outside if the tank is leaking.

4

Photograph the label

Take a picture of the data plate. It shows the size, fuel and model, which speeds up planning.

Choosing The Right Type Of Heater

There is no single right heater. The best choice depends on the fuel you already have, the space available and how much hot water your household uses.

Sizing Is Where Most Mistakes Happen

An undersized tank runs out in the middle of a shower. An oversized one costs more and wastes energy keeping extra water hot. The right size depends on how many people live in the house, how many bathrooms you have and whether you run a dishwasher and washer at the same time as showers.

We look at your current unit, your household and any big tubs or multiple showers, then recommend a size. We also check the first-hour rating, which is a better guide than the tank volume alone.

What A Full Replacement Includes

Removal and disposal

The old unit is drained, disconnected and carried out. You do not have to arrange hauling.

New connections

Fresh supply lines, shutoff valves and a proper drain and relief discharge so the new unit is code-ready.

Venting and gas

On gas heaters we check the vent pitch, the draft and the gas connection and correct what the code requires.

Expansion control

Where the system calls for it, we install an expansion tank so pressure swings do not stress the new heater.

Testing and walkthrough

We fill, purge, light or power the unit and check for leaks. Then we show you the shutoffs and the temperature setting.

Same-Day Or Planned: Your Timeline

Many replacements can be done in a single visit when the right unit is available. Same-day appointments depend on availability, so call early and tell us it is an emergency. If you need a specialty model such as a heat pump unit, we may stage the work so you get hot water first and the permanent unit follows.

If you are replacing a heater before it fails, the planned route is cheaper and calmer. See water heater installation for scheduled work on units that are still running.

Fuel Choices For A Cape Cod Home

Not every street in town has natural gas. Where gas is available, the utility serving Cape Cod customers is National Grid, and a gas heater can be the lower-running-cost choice. Homes without gas service usually run electric, propane or an indirect heater off an oil or gas boiler.

If you are unsure whether gas reaches your street, call your gas utility and ask. If you are on propane, the tank and regulator belong to your supplier, so we coordinate around them. Switching fuel is possible, but it changes the venting, the gas or electrical supply and the price, so we lay that out before you commit.

A heater in an unheated crawl space or garage needs extra thought on Cape Cod. Freezing temperatures can reach exposed supply lines and drain valves, so insulation and placement matter for how the new unit performs through winter.

Questions Worth Asking Any Plumber Before Replacement

  • What size and type are you recommending, and why that one?
  • Does the price include removal of the old unit, new shutoffs and relief piping?
  • Is a permit needed, and who pulls it?
  • What warranty comes with the unit, and who handles a claim?
  • What changes will the venting or gas line need?

We answer all of these before work starts, and the answers go on the written price.

Making The New Heater Last

A new heater lasts longer when it is looked after. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment. Test the relief valve at the same time. Keep the area around a gas heater clear so air can reach the burner. Set the thermostat only as high as you need.

If you have a tank you want to keep going longer, ask us about the anode rod, a sacrificial part that protects the tank from corrosion. It is a small cost compared with replacing a tank early.
