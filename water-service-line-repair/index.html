Emergency Signs Of A Failed Service Line

Your water service line carries water from the main in the street to your house. If it breaks, you may see a wet or sunken patch in the yard, hear water rushing with everything off or find the house suddenly without pressure. Treat those signs as urgent and call now.

Close the main shutoff valve inside the house, usually near where the line enters the basement or crawl space. That stops water inside, though it does not stop a leak in the buried section on the street side of your valve.

  • A soggy or sunken area in the lawn when it has not rained.
  • The meter spinning with every fixture closed.
  • A sudden drop in pressure at every tap.
  • Hissing or rushing sounds near where the line enters the house.
  • A water bill that jumps without a change in use.

Who Owns Which Part Of The Line

Responsibility for a service line is split between you and the water utility. Where the split falls depends on the utility, so we will not guess. Ask the Sandwich Water District which part of your line belongs to you before any digging begins.

The district keeps staff on call around the clock and its contact details are on its website. If the leak is at or before the meter, call them. If it is on your side, call us. We can help you work out which is which.

Spot Repair Or Full Replacement

If a single break is the only problem on a sound line, a spot repair can solve it. If the line is old, brittle or has failed before, a full replacement avoids repeated excavation. Modern lines are made of durable materials that resist corrosion, and the new connection to your house is built to last.

Spot repair fits when

  • One clear break in an otherwise sound line
  • The pipe material is in good shape
  • Access to the break is straightforward
  • The line has no history of failures

Replacement fits when

  • The line is old, corroded or lead suspect
  • Several leaks have occurred
  • Pressure is poor even after repair
  • The line material is outdated and fragile

Lead, Old Pipe And What To Ask About

The Sandwich Water District's 2025 report records a lead 90th percentile of 0 ppb in 30 sampled homes, with one site above the action level of 15 ppb. The district's own guidance says lead in tap water comes mainly from service lines and home plumbing.

If your house is old, it is reasonable to ask the district what is known about your line material. When we replace a line, we choose modern materials and tell you what was removed. The district can also explain testing options if you are curious about your own water.

Source: Sandwich Water District 2025 Water Quality Report and district FAQ.

Permits And Inspection In Sandwich

Plumbing work in Massachusetts follows the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and plumbing permits are issued only to licensed plumbers. The Sandwich Building Department handles plumbing and gas inspections and runs a Permit Eyes portal for permits. Inspections are held on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays, booked by phone.

Ask the Sandwich Building Department whether your line replacement needs a permit and inspection. We handle that process when it applies and tell you what to expect.

Sources: Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters and the Town of Sandwich Building Department.

Low Pressure Does Not Always Mean A Broken Line

Low pressure can come from a partly closed valve, a failing pressure reducing valve, clogged fixtures or a restricted old pipe. We check the simple causes first. If every tap is weak and the meter shows normal use, the service line itself may be narrowed by corrosion or buildup.

If you suspect a hidden leak instead, our leak detection service can pinpoint it without unnecessary digging.
